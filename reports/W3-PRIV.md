# W3-PRIV: strip congregant names from church transcripts

Branch `feat/w3-priv` (from `origin/main` 6da9756). Agent: Claude Code (Opus 5.5).
No church audio was fetched or processed. Every name in the eval sets and tests is invented or public.

## Result

**The targets are not met on blind data.** On the last blind set, scored once on frozen rules, recall on private names is **0.926** (target ≥ 0.98) and the false-redaction rate on biblical, historical and public names is **0.155** (target ≤ 0.02). The targets are met only on the sets the rules were tuned on. Do not index church audio on the strength of this module alone. Use it with the review step below.

### What fails on blind3 (frozen rules, not tuned on)

- **False redactions (55 words):** almost all are real public figures who are not on the hand-written public list: Mahalia Jackson, Lecrae, Maya Angelou, Phillis Wheatley, Fannie Lou Hamer, Anne Lamott, Chuck Colson, Francis Collins, Desmond Doss, Howard Thurman, Lamin Sanneh, Bartolomé de las Casas, Richard Dawkins and others. The rules treat a full name they do not know as private. That is the safe direction, and **40 of the 55 are listed by `privacy.review()`**, so a person can fix them with `allow`.
- **Misses (18 words):** mostly congregants who share a Bible or virtue name and appear right next to that Bible story, with no title, relation or prayer verb: "Rebecca on the third row", "Joseph in our men's group", "give joy to Joy", "Lord put Esther in the right place… like Queen Esther", "Hank and Orlando are serving". Only 1 of the 18 is flagged by `review()`. **These leaks are silent.** That is the main open risk.

## 1. What was done

- `src/preached/privacy.py`: `redact(words) -> words`, the exact signature of the W2-YT hook (`preached.youtube.privacy.redact`). It accepts:
  - W2-YT's frozen `Word(start, end, text)` dataclass;
  - W1-C's `(start, end, word)` tuples or lists (`align/asr_chunks.py`);
  - dicts with `"text"` or `"word"`.

  It returns the same records, in the same order, with the same times, and only the name letters replaced: `"Jane's,"` → `"[name]'s,"`. Input is not mutated. When nothing is redacted, it returns the input list itself, so W2-YT's `test_privacy_hook_is_identity` still passes against this module (checked with its `sample_words`).

  Also in the module:
  - `decide(texts)` gives one flag per word.
  - `redact_text(text)` handles plain text.
  - `review(words)` lists names a person should check before publishing, with times and a reason. It covers kept Bible or public names in a personal setting, and full names redacted only because they are not on the public list.
- **Approach: rules plus lists, no model.** Whisper capitalizes names, so the evidence is casing, the words around a name, and lists.
  - **Strong cues** make a name private, even a Bible name: titles ("Sister", "Mr.", "Pastor"), "my/our <relative>", prayer and announcement verbs ("pray for", "welcome", "thanks to", "baptizing"), "a young lady X", "the Hendersons".
  - **Weak cues** ("a man named", "his wife") lose to nearby Bible or public names.
  - A **lone given name** is redacted.
  - A **major Bible figure or public surname that is also a given name** (John, Mary, Lewis) is kept. The exceptions are a private event right after it ("John's surgery"), modern-life words with no scripture words around it ("Timothy had his scan Tuesday"), or "and" joining it to a private name ("Peter and Brenda").
  - **Public full names** are kept ("John Wesley", "C. S. Lewis"), and a first name coming back alone nearby is kept too ("John preached and Charles wrote the songs").
  - A **capitalized word on no list** that is not an English word is treated as a surname, unless it reads as a place ("at Flossenburg", "in Da Nang").
  - **Lowercase names** ("pray for brenda") are caught only right after a strong cue.
  - Accented names are folded for lookup (Müller, Søren, à Kempis).
- `src/preached/privacy_data/` (356 KB):
  - Generated by `scripts/build_privacy_lists.py` from public-domain sources:
    - `given_names.txt`: 13,128 SSA baby names, 1880–2017.
    - `bible_names.txt`: 2,673 proper nouns the KJV or WEB capitalizes mid-clause.
    - `lexicon.txt.gz`: 83,484 words from 12dicts 2of12inf plus Bible lowercase words.
  - Hand-written:
    - `bible_major.txt`: 135 figures.
    - `public_names.txt`: about 650 public and historical names.
    - `places.txt` and `terms.txt`: capitalized non-names.
- `privacy.example.toml`: per-library `allow` (pastor and staff who consent, matched as a whole run of words), `block` (always redacted), `drop` windows (seconds, optionally per `source` id) and `token`. Pass `config=` or set `PREACHED_PRIVACY=/path/privacy.toml`.
- Eval: four synthetic sets with 874 lines in total (`eval/privacy/README.md`), `evaluate.py`, frozen-run results in `eval/privacy/results/`, and `ner_baseline.py`.
- Tests: `tests/test_privacy.py` (23 tests).

## 3. Numbers

- **Accuracy:** see the table above. Per-category counts are in `eval/privacy/results/*.json`.
- **Review load on blind3:** 38 review items over 272 lines, about one per 7 sentences. Those sentences are deliberately dense with names, so a real sermon would produce far fewer.
- **Speed (one CPU core, Python 3.14):**
  - Import plus list load: 20–65 ms, +17 MB RSS.
  - Redaction: about 116,000 words/s (205k words in 1.8 s).
  - A 6,000-word sermon (about 40 minutes) takes 35 ms.
- **Tests:** 87 in the full suite (`uv run python -m unittest discover -s tests`), all passing: 64 existing and 23 new. One existing test logs an `EmbeddingMismatch` traceback on purpose.
- **NER baseline:** spaCy 3.8 (Explosion AI, Berlin). PERSON entities either alone or filtered by the same Bible and public lists. Recall / false-redaction:

| Set | Rules (frozen e980f36) | sm + lists | lg + lists | Rules ∪ lg | lg alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| blind2 (first run, 8b4372e rules) | 0.813 / 0.123 | 0.608 / 0.123 | 0.675 / 0.165 | 0.861 / 0.190 | 0.849 / 0.891 |
| blind3 | 0.926 / 0.155 | 0.632 / 0.141 | 0.769 / 0.161 | 0.955 / 0.178 | 0.893 / 0.935 |

NER alone redacts almost every Bible name. With the lists it misses far more congregants than the rules do. Combined with the rules, lg adds 7 private words on blind3 and costs 8 more false redactions. It also brings a roughly 300 MB environment (spaCy, thinc, numpy, plus a 12–560 MB model), runs about 25× slower, and still misses the target. The numbers don't justify adding it.

## 4. Open issues, risks, exclusions

- **Silent misses.** A congregant with a Bible or virtue name, mentioned in passing next to the Bible story of the same name, is kept and not flagged. The rule that would catch it ("redact every John") would wreck scripture narration. Before Gaston Community Church (or any church) audio is published, I recommend all three of:
  1. a person reads the `review()` list for each sermon;
  2. the church fills `block` with the members' names it knows are likely to come up (prayer list, staff, deacons). A `block` entry is redacted everywhere, including when it is a Bible name;
  3. the human approval step in AGENTS.md rule 3 includes a skim of the transcript.

  A stricter mode ("redact major Bible names unless the sentence is scripture") is an obvious knob, but I did not build or measure it after seeing blind3. It needs a fresh blind set.
- **Unknown public figures are redacted.** The list can't hold everyone. These are flagged by `review()`, and `allow` fixes them per library. Erring this way is deliberate.
- **Lowercase ASR names** are caught only after a strong cue ("pray for brenda"). Lowercase word-names (ray, june, sunny) are missed.
- **Synthetic data only.** The sets were written to be hard (dense traps, adversarial phrasing). Real sermons will likely have fewer traps per sentence, but that hasn't been measured, and no real congregant data may be used to measure it.
- **All four eval sets are synthetic and LLM-written** (dev by the author agent, blind1 and blind2 by separate Claude agents, blind3 by an unrecorded LLM). They share some habits with each other and with me. A human-written or human-audited blind set is pending. Label errors were found in blind1 and blind2, so blind3 likely has a few too. Its frozen score is reported as-is, with no label fixes.
- **Data sources:** SSA names via the `babynames` R package mirror, because ssa.gov returns 403 to scripts. KJV from Project Gutenberg #10, WEB from ebible.org, 12dicts from SourceForge. All are public domain. Raw downloads are in `/cache/privacy/raw` and are not committed.
- **`drop` windows** need the caller to pass `source=` (the W2-YT hook passes only `words`). Windows without a `source` apply to every input.

## 5. Dependencies added

None. The module uses only the standard library: `tomllib`, `gzip`, `unicodedata`, `re`, `dataclasses`. spaCy was installed only in a throwaway venv (`/cache/venvs/w3-priv-ner`) to measure the baseline, and is not in `pyproject.toml` or `uv.lock`. `rdata` and `pandas` were used once through `uv run --with` to convert the SSA `.rda` file. They are not dependencies either. No PRC-developed model was used or considered. spaCy's English pipelines come from Explosion AI (Germany).
